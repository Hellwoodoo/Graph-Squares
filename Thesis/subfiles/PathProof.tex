\begin{lemma}[Propagation] \label{lemma::propagation}
  Let $G$ and $T$ be graphs such that $G=T^2$ and $T$ is a tree. Let $s,u,v \in V_G$ be identified such that $s$ is a leaf, $u$ is its stem, and $v$ is the external vertex, with
  \[
  x_{su}=x_{us}=x_{uv}=x_{vu}=1.
  \]
  Then repeated application of path propagation assigns the integral edge assignments throughout $T$, recovering the tree root uniquely up to isomorphism.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Since $s$ is a leaf and $u$ is a stem of $T$, the path $s-u-v$ is contained in $T$. Let $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_r\}$ denote the vertices adjacent to $v$ in $T$ other than $u$. For each $a_i\in A$, the vertices $s,u,v,a_i$ form a $P_4$ in $T$. Since $x_{su}=1$, Lemma~\eqref{lemma::0_path_propagation} gives
  \[
  x_{ua_i}=0 \quad \text{ for all } a_i \in A.
  \]
  Let $B=\{b_1, \ldots, b_s\}$ denote the vertices adjacent to $A \backslash \{v\}$. Without loss of generality consider the vertex $a_1$ whereby $x_{ua_1}=0$ and $g_{ua_1} = 1$. Using Equation~\eqref{eq::IP_indicator_arg}
  \[
  1 \leq x_{ua_1} + \sum_{k \in V_G} y_{uka_1} = y_{usa_1} + y_{uua_1} + y_{uva_1} + \sum_{m \in \{a_1, \ldots, a_r\}}y_{u m a_1} + \sum_{n \in \{b_1, \ldots, b_2\}}y_{u n a_1}, 
  \]
  where since $g_{sa_1} = g_{uu} = 0$, Lemma~\eqref{lemma::g_ab=0} gives that $x_{sa_1} = x_{uu} = 0$ such that $ y_{usa_1}  = y_{uua_1} = 0$ by Equations~\eqref{eq::IP_product_upper_first}--\eqref{eq::IP_product_lower}. Likewise for all $m \in \{a_1, \ldots, a_r\}$, $y_{u m a_1} = 0$ since $x_{u m} = 0$, and for all  $n \in \{b_1, \ldots, b_2\}$, $y_{u n a_1} = 0$ since $g_{u n} = 0$ and therefore $x_{u n} = 0$. 
  Combining thus gives 
  \[
  1 \leq y_{uva_1}, 
  \]
  where $y_{uva_1} \leq x_{uv} = 1$ and $y_{uva_1} \leq x_{va_1}$ necessitates $x_{va_1} = 1$.   

  More generally, whenever two consecutive tree edges have been fixed
  to $1$, the $0$ Path Propagation lemma eliminates the corresponding
  root edges across the remaining length-$3$ paths. For every tree edge
  whose alternative explanations have thereby been eliminated, the
  product constraints imply that all corresponding $y$-variables are
  zero. The indicator equation then contains only the tree-edge variable,
  and Lemma~\eqref{lemma::1_path_propogation} forces that variable to
  equal $1$.

  Thus, the two propagation lemmas operate successively: integral
  assignments of tree edges produce zero assignments for incompatible
  root edges through Lemma~\eqref{lemma::0_path_propagation}, and these
  zero assignments eliminate the corresponding $y$-variables through
  Equations~\eqref{eq::IP_product_upper_first} and
  \eqref{eq::IP_product_upper_second}. Once all alternative
  explanations for a remaining edge of $G$ have been eliminated, its
  root-edge variable is the only nonzero term in
  Equation~\eqref{eq::IP_indicator_arg}, and is therefore forced to
  equal $1$.

  Since $T$ is finite, this process terminates after all edges of $T$
  have been identified. The resulting integral solution corresponds to
  the tree root $T$. Hence the propagation process recovers the tree
  root uniquely up to isomorphism.
\end{proof}
