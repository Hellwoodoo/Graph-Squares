\begin{theorem} \label{theorem::unicliqual_vertex}
  If a square graph $G$ has a tree root $T$, $\max \mathcal{UVLP}(G)$ returns an integral solution. 
\end{theorem} 
\begin{proof} 
    Assume for contradiction that there exists a square graph $G$ with a tree root $H$ such that the linear program has an optimal, maximal solution $x^*$ that is fractional. Then there exists at least one edge $ij \in E_G$ such that $0 < x^*_{ij} < 1 $. Consider the root solution $x^*$ and the following cases. 

    Let $i$ be a leaf, $j$ be its neighbour with degree $2$ and let $k \neq i$ be the other neighbour of $j$. Let $ij$ be the fractional edge along the path $i - j - k$ which meets the rest of the graph only at $k$. In $G$, $ijk$ form a clique in $G$ which by construction is also the only triangle that $i$ can be in. As the linear program applies Equation~\eqref{eq::single_triangle_fix}, $ij$ pendant edge will be fixed and hence cannot be integral, hence this case cannot occur. 

    Let $i$ be a leaf, $j$ be its neighbour with degree $\geq 2$ and let $k \neq i$ be another leaf of $j$. Let $j$ meet the rest of the graph. Then $ijk$ form a clique and solution $x^*$ can be extended by simply adding an additional edge $ik$ to form a root of larger size. This contradicts the maximality of $x^*$. 

    The remaining case is where both $i$ and $j$ are internal vertices. Denote the subgraph of $x^*$ from $i$ as $H_i$ and the subgraph from $j$ as $H_j$. Since $x^*$ must be a tree, there are no cycles in $H$ and hence $H_i$ and $H_j$ are disjoint with only one fractional bridge $ij$ connecting them. Consider $H_i, H_j$ and recursively apply the above logic such that we are eventually left with at least two subgraphs $H_0, H_1$ that do not have fractional bridges and are comprised completely of pendant vertices and leafs. From above two cases, all edges in the roots of $H_0, H_1$ are integral, and since the situation where a vertex has multiple leaves can not occur (there must be pendant 2-leaves together with leaves else would have above case), this completely categorizes the position of the node which connects to the rest of the graph such that there are no other automorisphsm which form the same square. As this holds for both $H_0$ and $H_1$, the endpoints of the bridge between them can be completely identified such that their bridge will be made integral. Applying this inductively thus means that $H_i$ and $H_j$ are completely integral such that $i$ and $j$ are identified and $ij$ is made integral. This contradicts the fractional nature of $x^*_{ij}$. 

    Since no fractional, solution exists, the optimal, maximal output of the linear program must be completely integral if it results in a tree root. 
\end{proof}
